## Executive Summary

- The standard Rubik's Cube color scheme places Blue opposite Green, Orange opposite Red, and Yellow opposite White.
- The Western color scheme (BOY: Blue-Orange-Yellow) is the most widely used arrangement on Rubik's Cubes and most cube-shaped twisty puzzles.
- The Western scheme is also called "Minus Yellow" because adding or subtracting yellow from any side's color yields its opposite.
- The Western scheme is the original scheme, first used on cubes produced and sold in Hungary; the original Ideal scheme had Yellow-Up with Red-Right and Blue-Back, while the current scheme has White-Up for logo visibility.
- The Japanese scheme differs from the Western scheme, with Blue and White opposite each other.

## Findings

### Standard Color Oppositions
In the standard Rubik's Cube color scheme, Blue is opposite Green, Orange is opposite Red, and Yellow is opposite White. The blue/green pair is always on opposite sides. A standard Rubik's Cube features six colors: white, yellow, red, orange, blue, and green.

### Western (BOY) Color Scheme
The Western color scheme, also known as BOY (short for Blue-Orange-Yellow), is the most used color arrangement on Rubik's Cubes and on the majority of cube-shaped twisty puzzles. In this scheme, Blue-Orange-Yellow go clockwise around a corner. With yellow on top and blue facing you, orange is to the left of blue and red is to the right of blue.

### "Minus Yellow" Naming
The Western color scheme is also called "Minus Yellow." This name refers to the relation between opposing sides' colors: if you add or extract yellow from any side, you get its opposite. Specifically, white + yellow = yellow, red + yellow = orange, and blue + yellow = green.

### Historical Origin and Scheme Evolution
The Western scheme is the original scheme. The first series of cubes were colored according to the Western scheme when those cubes were produced and sold only in Hungary. The original Ideal scheme had Yellow-Up with Red-Right and Blue-Back. The current scheme has White-Up, which makes the logo visible.

### Japanese Color Scheme
In the original Japanese scheme, blue and white are opposite each other. This differs from the Western scheme, where blue is opposite green and white is opposite yellow.

### Unusual Color Arrangements
The yellow and white sides being next to each other is described as an unusual color scheme.

### Low-Light Distinction
The most intelligent designs make it possible to distinguish opposite colors in low light.
