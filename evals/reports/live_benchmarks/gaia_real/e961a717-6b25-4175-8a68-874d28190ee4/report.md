## Findings

### Current Asian Monarchies
According to Wikipedia's 'Monarchies in Asia' article, the current Asian monarchies are Bahrain, Bhutan, Brunei, Cambodia, Japan, Jordan, Kuwait, Malaysia, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Thailand, and the United Arab Emirates. This list comprises 13 countries.

### Landlocked Asian Countries
There are 12 countries in Asia that do not have direct ocean access. The list of these landlocked Asian countries includes Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Tajikistan, Nepal, Mongolia, Turkestan, Kazakhstan, Laos, Bhutan, Azerbaijan, Armenia, and Afghanistan. **Qualification:** The inclusion of 'Turkestan' in this list is problematic, as Turkestan is a historical region rather than a recognized sovereign state. The claim should be understood with this caveat.

### Constitutional Monarchies in Asia
Bhutan, Cambodia, Japan, and Thailand have constitutional monarchies where the monarch has a limited or ceremonial role. This is supported by both the aalep.eu source and the broader evidence.

### Historical Transitions of Monarchies
Thailand changed from a traditional absolute monarchy into a constitutional one in 1932, while Bhutan made the same transition in 2008. Cambodia had its own monarchy after independence from the French Colonial Empire, which was deposed after the Khmer Rouge came into power. The Cambodian monarchy was subsequently restored in the peace agreement.

### Geographic and Demographic Superlatives
Kazakhstan is the world's largest landlocked country by area. Kyrgyzstan is the farthest landlocked country from any ocean. Ethiopia is the world's most populous landlocked country.

### Non-Sovereign Asian Monarchs
The Wikipedia article 'List of current non-sovereign Asian monarchs' covers monarchs in Bangladesh, India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, Timor-Leste, and the United Arab Emirates.

### Bhutan's Geography and Trade Access
The Kingdom of Bhutan has exactly two neighbors and no room for a third: China to the north and India everywhere else. Bhutan reaches through friendly territory for access to the sea, with an agreement to route its maritime trade through the Port of Mongla in Bangladesh.
