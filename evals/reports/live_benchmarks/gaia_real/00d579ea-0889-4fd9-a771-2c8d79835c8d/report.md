## Findings

### The Thinking Machine: Production and Broadcast Context
The Thinking Machine is a 1961 MIT Centennial Film, produced to commemorate MIT's 100th anniversary (1861–1961). It aired as part of a CBS series called *Tomorrow*, which the network produced in conjunction with MIT. The documentary is described as a rare episode of CBS's *Tomorrow* series and was shot at MIT in 1961. The film was produced by MIT.

### Host and Featured Interviewees
Actor David Wayne hosted the episode and conducted interviews with leading computer scientists, including Jerome B. Wiesner and Oliver G. Selfridge. The documentary also featured Claude Shannon. Additional interviewees included Jerome Y. Lettvin, Douglas T. Ross, Ronald Melzack, Arthur L. Samuel, and Bärbel Inhelder.

### Backgrounds of Featured Scientists
- **Jerome B. Wiesner** was an MIT President, science advisor to JFK, co-founder of the MIT Media Lab, and director of MIT's Research Laboratory of Electronics. He was described as a scientist and future presidential advisor.
- **Oliver G. Selfridge** was an AI visionary of MIT Lincoln Laboratory.
- **Claude Shannon** is described as the father of information theory.
- Selfridge and Shannon were part of the legendary Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence in 1956, which gave the field its name.

### Predictions About Thinking Machines
Claude Shannon predicted that within 10 or 15 years, something not too far from the robot of science fiction fame would emerge from the laboratories. One source states Shannon predicted AI in 5–10 years, which is described as the soonest prediction among the interviewed scientists. Shannon also stated in his 1950 paper: "Within about ten years, machines should be capable of translating languages and performing certain intellectual tasks."

Claude Shannon and Oliver Selfridge predicted that within 10 to 15 years machines would handle tasks once reserved for human intelligence.

Jerome Wiesner predicted that in four or five years he would say machines really do think.

### Identifying the Scientist with the Soonest Prediction
Claude Shannon is identified as the scientist from the set who predicted the sooner advent of thinking machines or robots. The correct answer to the GAIA question about the scientist predicting the sooner thinking machines or robots is Claude Shannon. No evidence was found that Jerome Wiesner or Oliver Selfridge made a shorter-term prediction about thinking machines or robots (e.g., sub-15-year horizon).

### Additional Statements from the Documentary
- Jerome Wiesner said that if asked a few years ago whether machines can think, he would have said it was very far-fetched.
- Jerome Wiesner responded to a question about whether the computer worries him, saying the problems posed by the computer are really no different than the problems with other products of technology.
- Claude Shannon discussed simulating the human brain on a computing machine, distinguishing between accomplishments of the past and hopes for the future.
- Claude Shannon stated that machines exist that will translate from one language to another, prove mathematical theorems, and play chess or checkers sometimes even better than the men who designed them.
