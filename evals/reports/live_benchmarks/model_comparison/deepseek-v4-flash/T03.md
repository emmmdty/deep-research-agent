## Executive Summary

- **GPT-4o and GPT-4o-Mini** are unified transformer-based multimodal models supporting text, vision, and audio, with GPT-4o offering a 128K context window and advanced multimodal attention mechanisms.
- **Gemini 2.5 models** are sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) transformers with native multimodal support, 1M token context windows, and state-of-the-art performance on coding, reasoning, and video understanding benchmarks (84.8% on Video-MME).
- **Qwen2.5-VL** is a flagship vision-language model with dynamic resolution processing, absolute time encoding, and capabilities spanning document parsing, object localization, and long-video understanding.
- **Video-MME** is a comprehensive video understanding benchmark featuring 900 videos, 254 hours of content, and 2,700 human-annotated QA pairs across 6 primary visual domains.
- **Claude models** lead in coding benchmarks (Sonnet 4 at 72.7% on SWE-bench) and safety evaluations, while pricing varies significantly across providers with batch discounts of 40-50%.

## Findings

### GPT-4o and GPT-4o-Mini Architecture

GPT-4o-Mini employs a unified, transformer-based autoregressive architecture supporting joint input/output over text, vision, and audio modalities. It is described as a compact multimodal language model integrating these modalities using a unified transformer architecture. GPT-4o is OpenAI's flagship omni-modal model combining text, vision, and audio in a unified architecture, offering a 128K context window with efficient token usage. GPT-4o features real-time responsiveness with superior performance across diverse tasks including reasoning, coding, multilingual understanding, and creative writing. GPT-4o incorporates advanced multimodal attention mechanisms that enable the model to selectively focus on relevant information across different modalities; these mechanisms are crucial for facilitating cross-modal interactions and ensuring effective integration and reasoning about information from multiple sources. GPT-4 is a separate multimodal large language model from OpenAI that accepts both text and images as input.

### Gemini 2.5 Architecture and Capabilities

Gemini 2.5 models are sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) transformers with native multimodal support for text, vision, and audio inputs. The efficiency of the sparse MoE architecture contributes to significantly improved performance over Gemini 1.5, with Google overcoming challenges in training stability of MoE architectures. Sparse MoE models activate a subset of model parameters per input token by learning to dynamically route tokens to a subset of parameters (experts), allowing decoupling of total model capacity from computation and serving cost per token. Gemini's architecture is based on early fusion, where image pixel patches, video temporal frames, audiograms, and text tokens are projected into a unified latent space. The Gemini 2.5 technical report describes the multimodal token processing approach as 'Unified Multimodal Token Interleaving.' Since all tokens from different modalities are processed within a shared sequence, standard self-attention mechanisms naturally provide cross-modal integration at every layer.

The Gemini 2.X model family includes Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 2.0 Flash, and Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite. Gemini 2.5 Pro is Google's most capable model, achieving state-of-the-art performance on frontier coding and reasoning benchmarks. It is a thinking model that excels at multimodal understanding and can process up to 3 hours of video content. Gemini 2.5 Pro has an LMArena score over 120 points higher than Gemini 1.5 Pro and supports long context input sequences of up to 1M tokens. Gemini 2.5 Flash provides excellent reasoning abilities at a fraction of the compute and latency requirements of Gemini 2.5 Pro, and is a hybrid reasoning model with a controllable thinking budget. Gemini 2.0 Flash is a fast and cost-efficient non-thinking model for everyday tasks, while Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite is the fastest and most cost-efficient model in the family, built for at-scale usage. The Gemini 2.X family covers the whole Pareto frontier of model capability vs cost.

Gemini 2.5 Pro supports an input length of 1M tokens and an output length of 64K tokens, with text and audio output modalities (audio output currently limited to Experimental or Preview). It supports tool use and dynamic thinking. Gemini 2.5 Pro did not reach any of the Critical Capability Levels in CBRN, cybersecurity, machine learning R&D, or deceptive alignment. The Gemini 2.5 family maintains robust safety metrics while improving dramatically on helpfulness and general tone compared to 2.0 and 1.5 counterparts. Both Gemini 2.5 Pro and Flash can process long-form text, whole codebases, and long-form audio and video data. Gemini 2.5 vision processing improvements allow processing of 3-hour-long videos. The smaller models in the Gemini 2.5 series use distillation. The pre-training dataset includes publicly available web documents, code, images, audio, and video, with a cutoff date of January 2025.

Gemini 2.5 is the first model family trained on TPUv5p architecture, using synchronous data-parallel training over multiple 8960-chip pods distributed across multiple datacenters. Training uses slice-granularity elasticity, resulting in tens of seconds of lost training time per interruption compared to 10+ minutes without elasticity, continuing at around 97% throughput while the failed slice recovers. During the training run, around 0.25% of steps were replayed due to suspected silent data corruption (SDCs), with 6% of these replays turning out to be genuine hardware corruption. Training uses split-phase SDC detection, where accelerators exhibiting intermittent SDCs are identified within minutes and quickly excluded from the job.

### Qwen2.5-VL Capabilities

Qwen2.5-VL is the latest flagship model of the Qwen vision-language series, demonstrating significant advancements in both foundational capabilities and innovative functionalities. It achieves a major leap forward in understanding and interacting with the world through enhanced visual capabilities, including enhanced visual recognition, precise object localization, robust document parsing, and long-video understanding. Qwen2.5-VL can precisely locate objects using bounding boxes or points, and provides robust structured data extraction from invoices, forms, and tables, along with detailed analysis of charts, diagrams, and layouts.

Qwen2.5-VL introduces dynamic resolution processing and absolute time encoding, enabling it to handle images of different sizes and videos lasting several hours, with second-level event localization capability. It trains a native dynamic resolution Vision Transformer (ViT) from scratch combined with Window Attention, maintaining native resolution while reducing computational overhead. Qwen2.5-VL excels not only at static image and document understanding but also serves as an interactive visual agent, performing reasoning, tool use, and task execution in real-world scenarios such as operating computers and mobile devices.

Qwen2.5-VL offers three sizes to meet various use cases from edge AI to high-performance computing. The flagship Qwen2.5-VL-72B model is particularly excellent at document and chart understanding, comparable to state-of-the-art models like GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet. It maintains robust language performance, preserving the core language capabilities of the Qwen2.5 LLM. The technical report was submitted to arXiv on February 19, 2025.

### Video-MME Benchmark

Video-MME is described as the first-ever comprehensive evaluation benchmark of multi-modal LLMs in video analysis. Video-MME-v2 is a comprehensive benchmark design introduced to address the gap between inflated leaderboard scores and real-world model capabilities in video understanding. Existing video understanding benchmarks are becoming increasingly saturated, exposing a critical discrepancy between inflated leaderboard scores and real-world model capabilities.

Video-MME features 900 videos totaling 254 hours with 2,700 human-annotated question-answer pairs across 6 primary visual domains and 30 subfields. It evaluates models across diverse temporal dimensions ranging from 11 seconds to 1 hour and integrates multi-modal inputs including video.

Gemini 2.5 Pro delivers state-of-the-art video understanding, scoring 84.8% on the Video-MME benchmark. Gemini 3 Pro has used Video-MME as the benchmark of video, and GPT-5 has used Video-MME as the benchmark of multimodal long context. Additional benchmark scores include: Gemini 1.5 Pro at 75.7% (no subs) and 81.6% (with subs); GPT-4o at 66.2% (no subs) and 65.8% (with subs); GPT-4V at 60.7% (no subs) and 63.7% (with subs); Qwen-VL-Max at 51.8% (no subs) and 51.7% (with subs); InternVL-Chat-V1.5 (20B) at 51.5% (no subs) and 53.2% (with subs); VideoLLaMA 3 (7B) at 66.2% (no subs) and 70.3% (with subs); LinVT (7B) at 70.3% (no subs) and 71.7% (with subs); TPO (7B) at 65.6% (no subs) and 71.5% (with subs); Long-VITA (14B) at 67.0% (no subs) and 72.0% (with subs); and Keye-VL-1.5 (8B) at 73.0% (no subs).

### MMMU Benchmark

The MMMU dataset is a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate multimodal models on college-level tasks requiring domain-specific knowledge and reasoning. It includes 11,500 questions sourced from college exams, quizzes, and textbooks, spanning six disciplines. The dataset covers 30 subjects and features 30 types of images, such as charts, diagrams, maps, tables, and more.

### Multimodal AI Challenges

The total cost of ownership of multimodal AI models is a major obstacle for many enterprises, including expensive computing hardware, specialized talent for development and supervision, API subscription fees, and ongoing model training and fine-tuning. Breakthroughs in multimodal AI models come from the adoption of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures. According to 2025 and 2026 industry analyses, MoE architectures allow AI systems to scale their total parameter count without increasing computational costs. The ability of multimodal AI to integrate and align diverse data sources at scale is the foundation of its transformative power. Managing multimodal data presents significant challenges for many teams, including fragmented storage, complex data integration workflows, and performance bottlenecks. Multimodal data management leads to data fragmentation, complex integration pipelines, performance bottlenecks at scale, and significant overhead in daily data management. Using pirated or unvetted datasets is increasingly classified as direct infringement. When unauthorized data is incorporated into training workflows, individual scraping also carries significant AI and copyright legal risks.

### Medical Image Registration

Deformable Image Registration (DIR) can benefit from additional guidance using corresponding landmarks in the images. The benefits of landmark guidance in DIR are largely understudied, especially due to the lack of automatic landmark detection methods for three-dimensional (3D) medical images.

### MMBench Quality Issues

In MMBench, some data samples might be wrong due to ambiguous questions or options, repeated options, or incorrect answers. MMBench wrong-question filtering was performed by inferring questions with three proprietary VLMs (GPT-4v, Gemini-Pro-V, Qwen-VL-Max) and two open-source VLMs (InternLM-XComposer2 and LLaVA-v1.5-13B).
