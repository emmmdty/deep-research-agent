## Executive Summary

- **Frontier benchmark leadership is fragmented across vendors**: Gemini 3.1 Pro leads GPQA Diamond (94.3%), Claude Opus 4.6 leads SWE-bench Verified (80.8%) and HLE with tools (53.1%), while Claude Fable 5 leads HLE (53.3%) and SWE-bench Verified (95.0%) as of July 2026.
- **Open-weight models have substantially narrowed the gap with proprietary systems**: The benchmark gap has shrunk from 8% to 1.7% on some tasks, with open-weight models reaching within single digits of proprietary models on most benchmarks and closing the coding gap (MiniMax M2.5 at 80.2% on SWE-bench).
- **The open vs. proprietary gap persists on long-horizon agentic tasks**: Epoch AI estimates open-weight models lag the closed frontier by an average of four months (eight-point gap on ECI), and the frontier gap remains for long-horizon agents requiring error recovery and coherent multi-step work.
- **Benchmark interpretation requires caution**: SWE-bench Verified has training data contamination issues, scores depend heavily on agentic scaffolds, and evaluation conditions can shift scores by 5–15%.
- **Licensing varies significantly across open-weight models**: Apache 2.0 (no restrictions), Meta Community License (700M MAU threshold), MIT (DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-V4), and MiniMax Community License (commercial-use conditions) all apply to different models.

## Findings

### Frontier Model Benchmark Leadership

Gemini 3.1 Pro leads scientific reasoning with 94.3% on GPQA Diamond and abstract reasoning with 77.1% on ARC-AGI-2. Claude Opus 4.6 leads SWE-bench Verified at 80.8% and HLE with tools at 53.1%. As of July 2026, Claude Fable 5 leads frontier coding (SWE-bench Verified 95.0%) and HLE (53.3%). Claude Opus 4.6 also leads on coding (Arena code Elo 1548) and nuanced writing, while GPT-5.4 excels at structured reasoning and computer use (75% OSWorld, surpassing human expert baseline). Grok 4 leads HLE at 50.7%.

### Claude 4 Model Performance

Claude Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 are hybrid reasoning models offering two modes: near-instant responses and extended thinking. Without extended thinking, Claude Opus 4 scores 74.9% on GPQA Diamond, 87.4% on MMMLU, 73.7% on MMMU, 33.9% on AIME, and 79.4% on SWE-bench Verified with high compute. Claude Sonnet 4 scores 70.0% on GPQA Diamond, 85.4% on MMMLU, 72.6% on MMMU, 33.1% on AIME, and 80.2% on SWE-bench Verified with high compute. With extended thinking, Claude Opus 4 scores 72.5%/79.4% on SWE-bench Verified and 79.6%/83.3% on GPQA Diamond; Claude Sonnet 4 scores 72.7%/80.2% on SWE-bench Verified and 75.4%/83.8% on GPQA Diamond. Claude 4 models lead on SWE-bench Verified, and benchmark scores show the highest scores achieved with or without extended thinking. Claude Opus 4 and Sonnet 4 are 65% less likely to use shortcuts or loopholes than Sonnet 3.7 on agentic tasks susceptible to shortcuts. Both models are available on the Anthropic API, Amazon Bedrock, and Google Cloud's Vertex AI. Claude Opus 4 is priced at $15/$75 per million tokens (input/output); Claude Sonnet 4 at $3/$15. Both were introduced on May 22, 2025.

### DeepSeek V3 and R1 Performance

DeepSeek-V3 is a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language model with 671B total parameters with 37B activated for each token, adopting Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) and DeepSeekMoE architectures. It was pre-trained on 14.8 trillion tokens, with maximum context length extended to 32K in the first stage and 128K in the second stage. DeepSeek-V3 achieves 88.5 on MMLU, 75.9 on MMLU-Pro, and 59.1 on GPQA, outperforming all other open-source models on these educational benchmarks. Its performance is comparable to leading closed-source models like GPT-4o and Claude-Sonnet-3.5. DeepSeek-V3 achieves state-of-the-art performance on math-related benchmarks among all non-long-CoT models, even outperforming o1-preview on MATH-500, and is the top-performing model for coding competition benchmarks such as LiveCodeBench. DeepSeek-V3's HumanEval coding score is 82.6, surpassing GPT-4o and Claude 3.5. DeepSeek-V3 trails behind GPT-4o and Claude-Sonnet-3.5 in English factual knowledge (SimpleQA) but surpasses these models in Chinese factual knowledge. DeepSeek-V3's training cost was approximately 2.788M H800 GPU hours ($5.6M), with full training costs comprising 2664K for pre-training, 119K for context extension, and 5K for post-training. DeepSeek-V3-Base has emerged as the strongest open-source base model currently available, especially in code and math.

DeepSeek R1 achieves 90.8 on MMLU (Pass@1), 79.8% pass@1 on AIME 2024, 84.0 on MMLU-Pro (EM), 71.5 on GPQA Diamond, and 92.9 on MMLU-Redux (EM). DeepSeek R1 has 671B total parameters with 37B activated parameters (MoE architecture). DeepSeek-R1 is comparable to OpenAI-o1 on code tasks. DeepSeek-R1-Distill models are derived from Qwen-2.5 series (1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B) and Llama3.1-8B-Base and Llama3.3-70B-Instruct. DeepSeek-R1 series models support commercial use and allow for any modifications and derivative works, including distillation for training other LLMs. DeepSeek-R1 code repository and model weights are licensed under the MIT License.

### Llama 4 Model Performance

Llama 4 Maverick scores higher than Llama 4 Scout across benchmarks: 69.8% vs. 57.2% on GPQA, 61.2% vs. 50.3% on MATH, 85.5% vs. 79.6% on MMLU, 90.0% vs. 88.8% on ChartQA, 43.4% vs. 32.8% on LiveCodeBench, 73.7% vs. 70.7% on MathVista, 77.6% vs. 67.8% on MBPP, 92.3% vs. 90.6% on MGSM, 80.5% vs. 74.3% on MMLU-Pro, 73.4% vs. 69.4% on MMMU, and 31.7% vs. 31.5% on TydiQA.

### Open-Weight vs. Proprietary Gap

Open-weight models have closed the coding gap: MiniMax M2.5 hits 80.2% on SWE-bench. Open-source LLMs are now within single digits of proprietary models on most benchmarks. Over the past year, the benchmark gap has shrunk from 8% to just 1.7% on some tasks. February 2026 rankings show GLM-5, Kimi K2.5, and DeepSeek V3.2 all scoring in what was frontier-only territory a year ago. However, Epoch AI estimates that the best open-weight models have lagged the closed frontier by an average of four months since January 2026, equivalent to an average eight-point gap on its composite ECI. On the Intelligence Index, the best open-weight model scored 59.7 (Kimi K3 max) vs. 63.1 for the best proprietary model (Claude Opus 5), a 3.4-point gap. On the Coding Index, the gap is 2.1 points (76.2 Kimi K3 max vs. 78.3 GPT-5.6 Sol xhigh). On the Agentic Index, the gap is 2.1 points (57.1 Qwen3.8 2.4T A95B vs. 59.2 Claude Opus 5). On SciCode, the gap is 1 point (59% Kimi K3 max vs. 60% Claude Fable 5). On LiveCodeBench, the gap is 2 points (90% DeepSeek V3.2 Speciale vs. 92% Gemini 3 Pro Preview high). On AA-LCR (long-context reasoning), the gap is 0 points (83% both Kimi K3 max and Muse Spark 1.2 xhigh). On GPQA Diamond, the gap is 1 point (94% Qwen3.8 2.4T A95B vs. 95% Gemini 3.7 Flash high).

Public benchmarks may flatter open models because public test sets are easier to optimize against. Open models often look closest on code-generation tests, but that is not the same as replacing a frontier coding agent. For long-horizon agents, the frontier gap persists; error recovery and coherent work over many steps remain harder than producing one correct function. Proprietary LLMs maintain more stable performance at scale for extreme long-context and high reliability tasks, while open-source models offer only moderate performance in this area.

### Open-Source Adoption and Usage Trends

OpenRouter's 2025 State of AI study analyzed more than 100 trillion tokens across its own platform and found that proprietary models still served most tokens, while open-weight models grew to approximately one-third of usage by late 2025. A 2025 survey showed that only 30% of developers preferred proprietary technology, compared to 57% who favored open-source options. Of the foundation models newly released in 2023, 65.7% were open-source, compared to only 44.4% in 2022 and 33.3% in 2021. Nature warns that relying on proprietary models leaves science relying on corporations' benevolence—an unstable situation.

### Open-Source Advantages and Disadvantages

Open-source LLMs offer several advantages: greater control, cost savings, potential for faster inference speeds (control over hardware and optimization), no rate limits or usage restrictions, and suitability for private or air-gapped deployments. Open-weight APIs and self-hosted deployments can win on marginal cost, provider choice, cache economics, and the ability to optimize a stable workload. Open-source LLMs can be cheaper at scale than proprietary models. The 27B open-source model outperforms some larger proprietary models. Open-source LLMs are optimized for efficient inference across various hardware, from edge devices to cloud deployments.

However, open-source LLMs are not free—they just move the bill from licensing to engineering, infrastructure, maintenance, and strategic risk. They often lack the extensive resources and professional support that come with proprietary models, which can pose significant challenges for enterprise adoption. Open-source LLMs have slower updates and security patches compared to commercial alternatives. Most 'open-source LLMs' are really open-weight LLMs; open weights let you download and run model parameters, while fully open systems may also publish training code, data, evaluation harnesses, and a license compatible with the Open Source Definition.

### Model-Specific Open-Weight Releases

MiniMax-M3 is close to Opus 4.7 and GPT 5.5 on software engineering benchmarks, scores 93% on GPQA Diamond and 80.5% on SWE Bench, and is released under the MiniMax Community License, which carries commercial-use conditions. DeepSeek-V4-Pro is the flagship model for maximum reasoning, coding, and agentic performance, released under the MIT license, pre-trained on over 32T tokens with a one-million-token context window. DeepSeek-V4-Pro is one of the strongest open-source models for reasoning-heavy and coding-heavy workloads. DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Max outperforms all other open-source models on SimpleQA-Verified by around 20 absolute percentage points. In 1M-token settings, DeepSeek-V4-Pro only needs 10% of KV cache and 27% of single-token inference FLOPs than DeepSeek-V3.2. MiMo-V2.5-Pro is the flagship LLM for coding agents, complex software engineering, and long-horizon tool use.

### Benchmark Characteristics and Limitations

GPQA Diamond tests expert reasoning with PhD-level science questions in biology, chemistry, physics, contains 448 expert-level questions, and is approaching saturation among leading frontier models. SWE-bench Verified involves real software engineering tasks and is contamination-resistant, containing 300 real software engineering issues from open-source repos. However, SWE-bench Verified has training data contamination issues, and SWE-bench Pro is becoming a more reliable alternative benchmark. SWE-bench scores are highly dependent on agentic scaffolds (such as Claude Code, Codex CLI), so comparisons must be made under identical conditions. HLE is designed to stay unsaturated and is the current frontier difficulty benchmark. MATH benchmark contains 12,500 competition math problems across 5 difficulty levels, measuring competition-level mathematics across algebra, geometry, number theory, combinatorics, and calculus. GSM8K contains 8,500 linguistically diverse grade-school math problems and tests chain-of-thought reasoning; frontier models score above 95% on GSM8K. MT-Bench contains 80 multi-turn questions judged by GPT-4 on a 1-10 scale.

In 2026, older benchmarks like MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval are saturated: top models cluster at 88–99%, so they no longer differentiate. Benchmark scores are affected by evaluation conditions (such as prompt framework, few-shot count, chain-of-thought, scoring method), which can shift scores by 5–15%. Models that dominate leaderboards often perform poorly in production environments. Multiple-choice benchmarks reward test-taking heuristics rather than deep reasoning.

### Licensing Landscape

Apache 2.0 (used by Command R+, Mixtral 8x22B) imposes no commercial-use restrictions, no MAU thresholds, and requires only attribution and notice of modifications. The Meta Community License for Llama 4 permits commercial use but requires a separate license agreement for products exceeding 700 million monthly active users. Llama 3 was released in 2024/04 under the Meta Llama 3 Community License Agreement, which is free if you have under 700M users and prohibits using LLaMA 3 outputs to train other LLMs besides LLaMA 3 and its derivatives. Meta Llama 3 license requires that if monthly active users exceed 700 million in the preceding calendar month, the licensee must request a license from Meta, which Meta may grant in its sole discretion. GPL v3 and AGPL v3 require open-sourcing derivatives, while RAIL and custom AUP (Llama/Gemma) licenses do not. GPL v3 and AGPL v3 do not restrict commercial use, while RAIL and custom AUP licenses restrict specific uses. GPL v3 and AGPL v3 are not used by major commercial LLM providers, while custom AUP licenses (Llama/Gemma) are.

### Deployment Cost Considerations

In lightweight usage scenarios (500K tokens per day), 12-month total costs are: OpenAI API $1,260, Anthropic API $1,800, open-weight API $360, and local deployment (Ollama/consumer hardware) $6,457. Local deployment hardware/compute costs are $3,350, electricity $190, operations labor $1,800, and depreciation $1,117. Running one to two mid-range GPU instances 24/7 for open-source LLM inference costs $1,500–$3,000. Local LLMs are ideal for privacy, offline tasks, and zero cost, but 7B-scale local models lag frontier models by 10–20 percentage points in reasoning ability. Local models cannot access the internet. Local deployment is not suitable for more than 30 users. When speed matters more than privacy (such as real-time chat), cloud APIs should be used. When precision or real-time data is important, cloud APIs should be used.

### 2026 Model Landscape and Recommendations

In 2026, for frontier reasoning and coding, Claude Opus, GPT-5, and Gemini Ultra lead; for cost-efficient high volume, mid-tier models like Claude Sonnet and GPT mini win; and for private or air-gapped deployments, open models such as Llama and Qwen are best. No single model dominates every task; the optimal architecture in 2026 routes different requests to different models based on task complexity, latency requirements, and cost constraints. For high-stakes coding or autonomous changes, the recommendation is to start with a proprietary frontier model for maximum long-horizon reliability. GLM-5.2 consumed more output tokens per task than several open peers, so it can be inexpensive per unit of intelligence and relatively token-inefficient at the same time.
