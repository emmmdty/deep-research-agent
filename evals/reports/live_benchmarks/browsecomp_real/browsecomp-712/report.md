## Findings

### National Team Coach Departures and World Cup Results
Julian Nagelsmann became the eighth national team coach to leave or be dismissed following elimination from the FIFA World Cup. He stepped down after Germany suffered a Round of 32 exit, losing 4-3 on penalties to Paraguay after the match ended 1-1.

### Norway's Return to Major Tournament
Norway qualified for the FIFA World Cup, breaking a 26-year curse. A two-part documentary presents an in-depth look at the return of Norway's men's national team to a major international tournament after 26 years.

### Senegal Coach Petition
Thousands of Senegalese have signed a petition calling for the dismissal of national team coach Pape Thiaw.

### Herbert Hoover's Early Life and Education
Herbert Hoover was born in an Iowa village in 1874 and grew up in Oregon. He was the son of Jesse and Hulda Hoover, and his father was a Quaker blacksmith. Hoover was a member of the first class at Stanford University (1895). He enrolled at Stanford University when it opened in 1891, graduating as a mining engineer. He graduated with a degree in geology and became a mining engineer.

### General Foods and Cheek-Neal Acquisition
General Foods' most important acquisition was Cheek-Neal in 1925. Joel Cheek of Nashville had developed a system for vacuum packing pre-ground coffee. Maxwell House Coffee, and later Sanka and Yuban, were to become the most important single part of the company.

### Early Women's Football in Australia
Women's football started in Australia in 1921. The first official women's football organisation in Australia was the Queensland Ladies' Soccer Association (QLSA), formed on 8 July 1921. William Betts was selected as the first coach of the QLSA. The QLSA was chaired by Mr. R.J. Powell, an early women's football ally who had helped found the Latrobe Ladies' Soccer Club in Queensland. The first official game of women's football in Australia took place on September 24, 1921, at the Brisbane Cricket Ground, with North Brisbane defeating South Brisbane 2-0. 10,000 people attended this first official game.

### Women's Football in England and the FA Ban
The Dick, Kerr Ladies team raised hundreds of thousands of pounds to support unemployed and disabled ex-servicemen who had returned from the battlefields of World War I. On Boxing Day of 1920, 53,000 people watched the Dick, Kerr Ladies take on St Helens Ladies at Goodison Park in Liverpool. In December of 1921, the English Football Association banned women from playing on association pitches altogether. The FA's 1921 ruling stated that the game of football is quite unsuitable for females and should not be encouraged.

### International Bans and the Australian 'Shadow Ban'
Some countries such as Brazil, Germany, and Spain banned women outright from the game. The 'shadow ban' on women's football in Australia lasted for 50 years.

### Women's European Championship Pilot
In 1957, a pilot Women's European Championship was organised in Berlin by the International Ladies Football Association (ILFA).
