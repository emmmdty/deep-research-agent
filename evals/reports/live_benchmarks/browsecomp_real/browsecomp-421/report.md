## Findings

### Tellus Journal and Climate Change Publication Share
Tellus, a 'Quarterly Journal of Geophysics', was created by the Swedish Geophysical Society in 1949. During the decades 1940–1960, Tellus published some 20% of the climate change papers in the compiled bibliography, more than any other journal.

### Global Spread of the Modern Laboratory
During the first decades of the 20th century, the modern laboratory became a global institution. Around 1930, the Institute for Physiology at the University of Conception in Chile illustrated this global spread in the experimental life sciences. In the early twentieth century, GE was interested in improving incandescent lighting, and new laboratory members were hired to direct their energies toward that goal.

### Percy Lavon Julian: Life and Career
Percy Lavon Julian was born in Montgomery, Ala., on April 11, 1899. He graduated first in his class from DePauw University with a bachelor's degree in chemistry in 1920, earned a master's degree in chemistry from Harvard University in 1923, and was awarded a Ph.D. in 1931 (the university is not named in the evidence). In 1935, Julian and a colleague determined how to synthesize physostigmine, a plant compound found in Calabar beans. Also in 1935, Julian and Josef Pikl completed the total synthesis of a compound (the compound name is truncated in the evidence). Julian helped Pikl, a fellow student at the University of Vienna, come to the United States to work with him at DePauw. Julian died of liver cancer in 1975, at the age of 76.

### Steroid Chemistry Context
In the 1930s, chemists recognized the structural similarity of a large group of natural substances—the steroids. The medicinal potential of steroids was clear, but extracting sufficient quantities from animal tissue and fluids was prohibitively expensive.

### Sample Selection in Economics Field Experiments
Sample selection is challenging for all primary data collection work in economics, but the hurdles are greater for experiments conducted in the field. Numerous researchers have highlighted the challenges of representative samples in university laboratory settings, and how these might be amplified in field experiments.

### ClimEx Handbook and Virtual Laboratory Study
The aim of the ClimEx paper was to provide a handbook of standardized field and laboratory methods across the soil–plant–atmosphere continuum, applicable to a broad range of terrestrial ecosystem climate change studies. The handbook focuses mainly on plant communities and the other organisms with which they interact because of their dominance in terrestrial ecosystems. Separately, the data and findings from a virtual laboratory paper will be added to an ongoing study into how virtual laboratories might improve student skills and thinking.
